# Promo guidelines (15–45s)

**Job:** make the target viewer feel the problem, see the product solve it, and take one action.
**Success test:** after one viewing, the viewer can state the problem, the product, and what to do next.
**Format:** hook → problem → solution → (proof) → CTA. This is the PAS pattern (Problem, Agitate, Solve), compressed.

## Structure

| Beat | 30s example | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–3s | A pattern interrupt: a question, a surprising stat, or the pain shown visually. The product or problem must be visible, not a logo. |
| Problem | 3–8s | The pain in the audience's words. One scene, two at most. Agitate briefly with the cost of the status quo, in time, money or risk. |
| Turn | 8–10s | The product appears as the answer: "Meet Acme." |
| Solution | 10–24s | 2–4 benefits, one per scene (about 3–5s each). Each one shows the UI doing the thing as it's said. |
| Proof (optional) | 24–27s | One metric, a customer logo strip, or a short quote. Use only claims the user can substantiate. |
| CTA | 27–30s | One action plus a URL on screen, plus the logo. Hold for ≥3s. |

Scale proportionally for 15s or 45s. For 15s, drop proof and use 2 benefits.

## Script
- **Budget:** about 2.5 words/sec, so 30s is about 65–75 words including pauses. Write the 30s cut first, then derive the 15s cut by removing scenes, not by talking faster.
- **Hook formulas:**
  - "Still <painful thing>?"
  - "<Surprising number> of <audience> <pain>."
  - "Here's how <audience> <achieve outcome> in <short time>."
- **Benefit lines** follow "verb + outcome + qualifier": "Find any contract in seconds — even scanned PDFs."
- **End with the CTA spoken *and* shown.** Use an imperative verb: "Start free at acme.com."
- Avoid jargon, superlatives you can't prove ("the best"), and stacked adjectives.

## Visuals
- **Show the product within 10s, and ideally in the hook.**
- **Problem scenes can use non-product imagery,** such as messy spreadsheets, a cluttered inbox, or a stressed-user illustration. **Solution scenes must be real UI.**
- **One UI action per benefit scene.** Zoom to the region, animate the result appearing, and highlight the key element on the VO's emphasis word.
- **Visual contrast between problem and solution:** desaturated or busy for the problem, clean, bright and on-brand for the solution.
- Change the shot every 2–4s. Keep transitions consistent, and prefer cuts on the beat.

## Audio
- **Energy arc:** music builds from the problem into the solution and lifts at the turn. In the ElevenLabs Music prompt, ask for a "lift at <turn second>".
- Duck the music under the VO, then swell it in the CTA hold.

## Versions
- From one storyboard, render the 30s master, a 15s cut-down, and a 9:16 social version. Re-compose the frame for 9:16 with the UI crop centred and the text kept clear of platform overlays.
- **Landing-page embed:** autoplay muted with burned-in captions.

## Pitfalls
- Spending 10s on the problem before the product appears.
- A feature list instead of benefits.
- Multiple CTAs ("Visit our site, follow us, book a demo").
- Stock-footage montages that could belong to any company.
- Unverifiable claims ("10x faster"). Ask the user for the source, or cut the claim.

## Pre-render checklist
- [ ] Hook ≤3s, product visible by 10s.
- [ ] Problem → solution contrast is clear visually, not just verbally.
- [ ] ≤4 benefits, each with matching on-screen UI.
- [ ] Every claim is substantiated by the user.
- [ ] One CTA, spoken and shown, held ≥3s.
- [ ] Within the 15–45s target, and the 15s cut-down still makes sense.
